# Production Workflow

Use this workflow for each film. Adapt the steps when a project genuinely requires it, and record the reason.

## 1. Source and story

- Identify the biblical passage(s), translation, historical context, and relevant parallel accounts.
- Separate what the text explicitly says from interpretation and cinematic invention.
- Define the film's central question, point of view, emotional progression, and ending.
- Check chronology, geography, character motivation, and cause and effect.

## 2. Script and timing

- Draft the scene sequence before polishing dialogue.
- Estimate runtime and allocate time to emotional beats.
- Mark direct scripture quotations and their references.
- Review dialogue for historical tone, clarity, and textual accuracy.
- Lock the script only after story, source, and timing reviews.

## 3. Visual development

- Approve character reference sheets and costume descriptions.
- Approve location layouts, geography, and key props.
- Establish aspect ratio, lens language, lighting logic, color palette, and visual references.
- Create a continuity guide before generating final shots.

## 4. Storyboard and shot planning

For every shot, record:
- Shot ID and script beat
- Duration and framing
- Camera position, movement, and lens intention
- Character blocking, eyelines, and screen direction
- Performance and emotional subtext
- Location, lighting, props, and continuity dependencies
- Sound, transition, and edit point
- Generation method, reference inputs, and status

## 5. AI generation

- Test difficult shots before committing to a full sequence.
- Reuse approved character and location references.
- Generate shots individually or in controlled batches; do not rely on one long generation to solve continuity.
- Keep prompt versions and source references with each shot.
- Record model, version, settings, seed (when available), reference assets, and known defects.
- Review identity, anatomy, motion, costume, geography, lighting, and temporal consistency.

## 6. Edit and sound

- Assemble a rough cut to test story clarity and rhythm.
- Refine timing around performance, silence, sound cues, and visual matches.
- Use sound design and music to support the scene, not explain what the image already communicates.
- Review the cut without music to test whether the visual storytelling works.

## 7. Review and release

- Check scripture references, subtitles, names, chronology, and credits.
- Review continuity and technical quality at the intended delivery resolution.
- Mark approved assets and documents clearly.
- Record the final runtime, export settings, release files, and lessons learned.

## Production status

Use consistent statuses: `planned`, `in-progress`, `review`, `approved`, `blocked`, and `superseded`.
