# Visual Style Guidelines

These are the project's starting defaults, not rules that override a story's needs. Film-specific deviations must be recorded in that film's visual plan.

## Core look

- Live-action realism with a cinematic historical-film sensibility.
- Believable human faces, skin, hair, fabric, stone, wood, fire, and natural environments.
- Restrained performances and physically plausible movement.
- Layered naturalistic light; motivated practical sources where appropriate.
- Intentional composition, clear subject hierarchy, and controlled background detail.
- Atmosphere and emotion should come from framing, performance, light, sound, and editing rather than excessive visual effects.

## Default delivery

- Aspect ratio: **16:9 horizontal**, unless a film explicitly specifies otherwise.
- Plan framing with safe space for titles and platform crops when needed.
- Choose resolution and frame rate per delivery target and record them in the film's production notes.

## Continuity rules

- Keep character identity, apparent age, facial features, hair, facial hair, body proportions, and costume stable across connected shots.
- Keep location layout, entrances, exits, props, light direction, time of day, and weather consistent.
- Track screen direction, eyelines, axis, character positions, and prop hand positions.
- Do not add accessories or props by default. Every visible item should have a story or design reason.
- Avoid generic fantasy styling, artificial-looking glow, excessive slow motion, and spectacle that weakens the emotional truth.

## Prompt design

Prompts should describe the shot's purpose and observable details: subject, action, setting, framing, camera, lighting, atmosphere, motion, and continuity references. Keep stable character and location descriptions in reusable reference documents rather than rewriting them inconsistently in every shot prompt.
