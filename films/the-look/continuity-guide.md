# The Look — Continuity and Staging Guide

**Status:** Draft — spatial and visual references not yet approved  
**Purpose:** Prevent character, location, lighting, and screen-direction drift between generated shots.

## 1. Time structure

The film contains two distinct moments on the same night:

1. The upper-room declaration, before Jesus' arrest.
2. The high priest's courtyard, after Jesus has been arrested.

The cut between them must communicate elapsed story time through location and action, not through an explanatory caption.

## 2. Character continuity

### Peter
- Adult man; age, face, hair, beard, build, and costume remain to be approved in a dedicated reference sheet.
- The same person must be recognizable in the upper room and courtyard.
- Costume remains unchanged across the night unless a specific story reason is approved.
- Emotional progression: sincere conviction → alert unease → reflexive denial → defensive irritation → desperate denial → frozen recognition → grief.
- Avoid playing him as foolish, smug, or cowardly from the beginning.

### Jesus
- Adult man; face, hair, beard, build, and costume remain to be approved in a dedicated reference sheet.
- Same identity and clothing in the upper room and courtyard.
- In the courtyard, his physical condition should reflect arrest and custody without adding graphic injury not required by the story.
- Emotional performance is restrained; the look is direct and quiet.

### Supporting roles
- One servant woman and two adult men are required for the three challenges.
- They should look like ordinary people in a first-century Judean setting, not stylized villains.
- Approve distinct faces and costumes so the roles do not morph between shots.

## 3. Location A — Upper room

### Required geography
- A modest first-century room, not a grand fantasy hall.
- Low table and simple seating.
- One practical oil lamp is the key visible light source.
- Other figures may be present in soft focus but must not distract from Peter and Jesus.

### Lighting
- Warm, low, motivated lamp light.
- Natural falloff into the room's darker edges.
- Avoid excessive gold glow or artificial shafts of light.

## 4. Location B — High priest's courtyard

### Master layout to approve
Use a single top-down layout reference before generating coverage. It must define:
- Courtyard boundary walls and openings.
- Passage through which Peter enters and later exits.
- Fire position as the central visual anchor.
- Approximate positions of the servant woman and two men.
- The far-side route along which Jesus is staged as he is led through the courtyard.
- Camera axis for the master shot and the look/reverse-look coverage.

### Spatial continuity rules
- The fire remains in the same position in every shot.
- The entrance/exit passage must not jump to the opposite side.
- Peter's relation to the fire and passage remains consistent.
- Jesus' route must allow a believable turn toward Peter without crossing the established axis unpredictably.
- Crowd members may shift naturally but must not teleport or multiply between cuts.

## 5. Lighting continuity

- Night ambience is cool-neutral, not saturated blue.
- Firelight gives a restrained amber edge to faces nearest the fire.
- Faces farther from the fire fall naturally into shadow.
- No sudden sunrise, supernatural beam, halo, glowing eyes, or lighting change at the rooster's crow.
- The upper-room lamp and courtyard fire must remain visibly different practical sources.

## 6. Eyeline and screen direction

- Establish the courtyard geography with a master shot before close coverage.
- Peter's glance toward the passage must point toward the correct off-screen direction.
- Jesus' turn and Peter's reaction must share a consistent axis.
- The audience must understand that Jesus is looking directly at Peter, not past him.
- Do not flip footage horizontally as a shortcut without checking costume details and spatial anchors.

## 7. Props and wardrobe

- Keep objects historically plausible and visually restrained.
- No modern fasteners, synthetic fabrics, modern furniture, modern glassware, or decorative fantasy accessories.
- Do not add props to a character's hands unless the action requires them.
- Fire vessel, fuel, seating, lamp, and table must be consistent within each location.
