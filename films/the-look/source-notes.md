# The Look — Scripture Source Notes

**Status:** Working research note  
**Primary narrative account:** Luke 22:31–34, 54–62  
**Translation for exact quotations:** Not yet locked

## Primary source map

| Passage | What the text establishes | Film use |
|---|---|---|
| Luke 22:31–32 | Jesus speaks to Simon Peter about testing and says he has prayed for him; Peter is told to strengthen his brothers after turning back. | Optional thematic background. Not currently dramatized due to the short runtime. |
| Luke 22:33 | Peter says he is ready to go with Jesus to prison and to death. | Opening declaration. |
| Luke 22:34 | Jesus predicts that before the rooster crows that day, Peter will deny three times that he knows him. | Opening warning and remembered words after the crow. |
| Luke 22:54 | Jesus is arrested and brought to the high priest's house; Peter follows at a distance. | Establishes the transition from the supper to the courtyard. |
| Luke 22:55 | A fire is kindled in the middle of the courtyard; people sit together and Peter sits among them. | Courtyard fire and Peter's position among the group. |
| Luke 22:56–60 | A servant woman and two men challenge Peter; he denies knowing Jesus / being associated with him. | Three escalating denials. |
| Luke 22:60–61 | While Peter is still speaking, the rooster crows; the Lord turns and looks at Peter. | Sound-led cut and emotional center. |
| Luke 22:61–62 | Peter remembers Jesus' words, leaves, and weeps bitterly. | Recognition, exit, and ending. |

## Important textual distinction

The opening declaration is based on **Luke 22:33**. Do not replace it with the distinctive wording of Matthew 26:33 ("though all may fall away...") and then cite it as Luke.

The three challenges and denials should follow Luke's sequence. The exact wording varies among Chinese and English Bible translations. Until a translation is selected, dialogue in the screenplay is explicitly an adapted paraphrase.

## What the text does not specify

The Gospel account does not provide:
- A detailed architectural plan of the courtyard.
- Exact positions of Peter, Jesus, the servant woman, or the two men.
- A detailed description of Jesus' facial expression during the look.
- The exact duration of the eye contact.
- The precise camera-visible path Jesus takes across the courtyard.

These are film staging decisions. They should serve clarity and emotional truth without being presented as additional biblical facts.

## Parallel accounts — comparison only

Matthew 26:69–75, Mark 14:66–72, and John 18:15–18, 25–27 also describe Peter's denials, but their details and phrasing differ. The film's narrative spine remains Luke. If a detail from another account is later adopted, record it as a deliberate harmonization rather than silently blending accounts.
