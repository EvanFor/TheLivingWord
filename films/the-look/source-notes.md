# The Look — Scripture Source Notes

**Status:** Working research note — Draft 0.3  
**Primary narrative account:** Luke 22:31–34, 54–62  
**Greek text reference:** SBL Greek New Testament (SBLGNT)  
**Translation for exact Chinese quotations:** Not yet locked

## Research principle

The film follows Luke's account as its narrative spine. The Greek text is the primary control for meaning; Chinese and English translations are comparison aids, not authorities that override the source text.

Keep three categories separate:

1. **Textual observation:** what Luke's Greek text actually says.
2. **Interpretive conclusion:** what the passage most plausibly communicates in context.
3. **Film decision:** staging, performance, camera, sound, timing, and dialogue adaptation.

A film decision must never be presented as an explicit biblical detail.

## Greek observations worth preserving

### Luke 22:31–32 — plural and singular address

- In v.31, **ὑμᾶς (hymas)** is plural: "you" addressed as a group.
- In v.32, **σοῦ (sou)** is singular: "you" addressed to Peter personally.
- **ἐδεήθην (edeēthēn)** expresses petition or earnest request: Jesus says he has prayed for Peter.
- **ἐκλίπῃ (eklipē)** concerns failing, giving out, or ceasing; avoid flattening it into a claim that Peter will never experience a crisis of faith.
- **ἐπιστρέψας (epistrepsas)** means turning/returning; **στήρισον (stērison)** means strengthen or establish.

The sequence matters: Jesus anticipates Peter's failure and speaks of his return and future responsibility to strengthen his brothers.

### Luke 22:33–34 — Peter's confidence

Peter says he is ready (**ἕτοιμός εἰμι**) to go with Jesus to prison and death. The text presents a confident declaration. It does not explicitly say Peter is lying or consciously pretending.

The film should not portray the declaration as knowingly false. Peter's sincerity is a defensible dramatic interpretation; the exact psychology remains an acting choice.

### Luke 22:54–55 — distance and proximity

- **μακρόθεν (makrothen):** from a distance / far off.
- **μέσος αὐτῶν (mesos autōn):** among them / in their midst.

Luke gives a meaningful spatial contrast: Peter follows at a distance, then sits among the people gathered around the courtyard fire. The text does not state why he chooses that position or describe his internal thoughts.

### Luke 22:56–60 — the three denials

- In v.56, **ἀτενίσασα (atenisasa)** describes the servant girl's intent or fixed look at Peter. It is the girl's gaze, not Peter's.
- In v.57, **ἠρνήσατο (ērnēsato)** explicitly means he denied.
- In v.58, Peter answers **οὐκ εἰμί** ("I am not") to the claim that he is one of Jesus' followers. The object is understood from the accusation.
- In v.59, **διϊσχυρίζετο (diischyrizeto)** conveys insisting or strongly asserting. The accuser also identifies Peter as a Galilean.
- In v.59, **διαστάσης ὡσεὶ ὥρας μιᾶς** indicates that about an hour had elapsed.
- In v.60, **παραχρῆμα** means immediately, and **ἔτι λαλοῦντος αὐτοῦ** means while he was still speaking.

Do not claim that Luke describes Peter's accent as the reason he is recognized. Luke's stated reason in v.59 is that the accuser asserts Peter was with Jesus and says he is a Galilean. Mark has a more specific accent detail.

The three denials have different wording, but the text does not explicitly prescribe a three-stage psychological progression such as instinct, irritation, and panic. Such progression may be used as film interpretation, not as a lexical fact.

### Luke 22:61–62 — the look and the response

- **στραφείς (strapheis):** having turned / turning around.
- **ἐνέβλεψεν (eneblepsen):** looked at / fixed his gaze on.
- **ὑπεμνήσθη (hypemnēsthē):** remembered / was reminded.
- **ἔκλαυσεν πικρῶς (eklausen pikrōs):** wept bitterly.

Luke's narrative order is: the Lord turns and looks; Peter remembers the Lord's word; Peter goes outside and weeps bitterly.

The text does not define the precise emotion on Jesus' face, explain the physical distance between Jesus and Peter, or state how many seconds they look at one another. Do not turn a plausible interpretation into a claim about the Greek wording.

## Important textual distinctions

- The opening declaration is based on **Luke 22:33**. Do not replace it with Matthew 26:33's distinctive "though all may fall away..." wording and cite it as Luke.
- The three challenges and denials should follow Luke's sequence.
- Luke itself includes the third accuser's statement that Peter is a Galilean (22:59). The specific claim that Peter's accent gave him away is associated with Mark 14:70, not Luke's wording.
- Luke 22:60 explicitly places the rooster's crow while Peter is still speaking.
- Luke 22:61 explicitly says the Lord turned and looked at Peter. It does not specify Jesus' facial expression.
- Luke 22:62 says Peter went outside and wept bitterly. It does not describe additional gestures such as falling to his knees or striking his chest.

## What the text does not specify

The Gospel account does not provide:
- A detailed architectural plan of the courtyard.
- Exact positions of Peter, Jesus, the servant girl, or the two men.
- The precise camera-visible path Jesus takes across the courtyard.
- Jesus' exact facial expression during the look.
- The physical distance or exact duration of the eye contact.
- Peter's detailed internal thoughts at each denial.
- The precise volume, pitch, or emotional delivery of the denials.

These are film staging and performance decisions. They should serve clarity and emotional truth without being presented as additional biblical facts.

## Parallel accounts — comparison only

Matthew 26:69–75, Mark 14:66–72, and John 18:15–18, 25–27 also describe Peter's denials, but their details and phrasing differ. The film's narrative spine remains Luke. If a detail from another account is later adopted, record it as a deliberate harmonization rather than silently blending accounts.

## Translation policy

No Chinese translation has been selected as the sole authority for the film.

Before locking dialogue or on-screen verse quotations:
1. Compare multiple Chinese translations.
2. Check the Greek wording and immediate context.
3. Record whether each line is a direct quotation, close translation, or dramatic paraphrase.
4. Identify the translation whenever a displayed quotation follows a published Chinese Bible text.
5. Do not silently combine wording from different translations.

# 中文对照版 / Chinese Companion

## 研究原则

本片以路加福音为叙事主线，以 SBL 希腊文新约为语义核对依据。中文和英文译本用于比较，不应反过来取代原文。必须区分三类内容：经文直接记载、结合上下文作出的解释，以及为电影表达而作出的调度与创作决定。电影创作决定不能伪装成圣经明确记载。

## 关键希腊文观察

- **ὑμᾶς (hymas)**：22:31中的复数“你们”，面向群体。
- **σοῦ (sou)**：22:32中的单数“你”，转向彼得个人。
- **ἐδεήθην (edeēthēn)**：恳切祈求；耶稣说自己已经为彼得祈求。
- **ἐκλίπῃ (eklipē)**：衰败、失去力量或停止；不要简化成彼得永远不会经历信心危机。
- **ἐπιστρέψας (epistrepsas)**：回转、转回；**στήρισον (stērison)**：坚固、扶持。
- **ἕτοιμός εἰμι**：我已经准备好；强调准备状态，不只是愿意。
- **μακρόθεν (makrothen)**：从远处、远远地。
- **μέσος αὐτῶν (mesos autōn)**：在他们中间。
- **ἀτενίσασα (atenisasa)**：女仆定睛看彼得；不是彼得的目光。
- **οὐκ εἰμί**：字面为“我不是”，语境中否认自己属于耶稣的群体。
- **διϊσχυρίζετο (diischyrizeto)**：坚持、强烈断言。
- **ἔτι λαλοῦντος αὐτοῦ**：他还在说话的时候。
- **στραφείς (strapheis)**：转过身；**ἐνέβλεψεν (eneblepsen)**：看着、注视。
- **ὑπεμνήσθη (hypemnēsthē)**：想起、被提醒。
- **ἔκλαυσεν πικρῶς (eklausen pikrōs)**：痛哭、悲痛地哭泣。

路加没有说彼得因口音被认出；22:59确实写到指认者称彼得是加利利人。关于口音的更具体细节见马可福音，不应冒充路加原文。三次否认的心理递进可以作为表演设计，但不是希腊词汇本身规定的心理阶段。

## 经文没有说明的内容

路加没有提供院落建筑图、人物精确站位、耶稣经过院子的具体路线、耶稣当时的面部表情、彼此对视的距离与秒数，也没有说明彼得每次否认时的内心独白。这些属于电影调度和表演决定。

## 中文译本政策

尚未指定某个中文译本为唯一权威。锁定对白或屏幕经文前，应比较多个译本、核对希腊文和上下文，并明确标注每句话属于逐字引用、贴近原文的翻译，还是戏剧化意译。不得无说明地拼接不同译本。

## 其他福音书

马太福音 26:69–75、马可福音 14:66–72、约翰福音 18:15–18、25–27也记载彼得不认主，但细节和措辞不同。本片叙事主线仍为路加福音。若采用其他福音书的细节，必须明确记录为和谐处理，不可悄悄混用。
