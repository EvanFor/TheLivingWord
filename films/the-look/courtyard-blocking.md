# The Look — Courtyard Blocking Proposal

**Status:** Proposal — Draft 0.1, not approved  
**Purpose:** Establish one coherent courtyard geography for the storyboard, camera coverage, and later visual references.  
**Scripture anchor:** Luke 22:54–62  
**Important:** Luke does not provide this architectural plan or exact blocking. Every spatial arrangement below is a film proposal, not a claim about the biblical text.

## 1. Design goals

The courtyard must make five things legible:

1. Peter follows Jesus at a distance and enters the same courtyard.
2. Peter sits among the people gathered around the fire.
3. Three different people can challenge Peter without the group appearing to teleport or change size.
4. Jesus can be moved through the same courtyard and turn toward Peter at the moment of the look.
5. Peter can leave through a clear route after the look.

The layout should feel like a plausible first-century high-priestly residence: enclosed stone space, practical openings, a domestic fire, and a route into the inner house. Avoid a grand palace or a fantasy arena.

### Legend

- **[G] West gate / passage:** Peter's entry and exit route. Jesus is brought in through this gate before being led toward the inner house.
- **[D] Inner house door:** destination toward which Jesus is being escorted.
- **[F] Fire:** central visual anchor, consistent with Luke 22:55's fire in the middle of the courtyard.
- **[PETER] Peter's seat:** on the south-east side of the fire, among the gathered people but with a clear sightline toward Jesus' far-side route.
- **[SERVANT] Servant woman:** near the east side of the fire, positioned to see Peter in the firelight.
- **[M1] First man:** near the south-west group edge, able to identify Peter after the first denial.
- **[M2] Third accuser:** near the east/north-east side, able to make the final accusation after the time interval.
- **Camera zone:** proposed west/south-west side of the Peter–Jesus eyeline axis.

The exact number and placement of background people remain flexible. Do not overcrowd the courtyard or obscure the principal eyelines.

## 3. Blocking proposal

### Arrival and seating

1. Jesus is brought through the west gate under guard and moved toward the inner house.
2. Peter follows at a distance, entering through the same gate after the arrest party.
3. Peter pauses at the courtyard edge before approaching the fire.
4. He sits on the south-east side of the fire, among the gathered people.
5. The servant woman notices him from the east side.

Peter's hesitation and route to the fire are staging choices. Luke states that Peter followed at a distance and later sat among them; it does not describe each step.

### Three challenges

- **First challenge:** the servant woman remains near the east side of the fire. She looks toward Peter in the firelight. Her line should be directed to the nearby group or spoken while indicating Peter, matching Luke's third-person wording: "这个人也跟他在一起。"
- **Second challenge:** the first man is close enough to address Peter directly: "你也是他们当中的一个。" Peter answers: "我不是。"
- **Third challenge:** after the textual interval of about an hour, the second man speaks with greater certainty. His accusation follows Luke's wording and perspective: "这人确实和他在一起，因为他也是加利利人。"

The one-hour interval is part of Luke's account. The film may compress it, but must not imply that all three challenges happen in one uninterrupted exchange. A change in background activity, fire level, or sound bed can suggest elapsed time without a title card.

### The look

1. At the third denial, Peter is near the fire on the south-east side.
2. Jesus is being escorted along the far-side route toward the inner house door.
3. Before reaching the door, Jesus turns toward Peter.
4. Peter looks up and meets Jesus' eyes across the courtyard.
5. Keep the route, eyelines, and camera axis consistent. The fire remains the spatial anchor but should not obscure the faces or the eye contact.

Jesus' movement across the courtyard and the exact point where he turns are invented staging. Luke only states that the Lord turned and looked at Peter.

### Peter's exit

Peter lowers his eyes, turns away from the fire, and exits through the west gate/passage. He takes the south-side route around the fire so that the audience can read his departure without crossing through the gathered group.

The exact exit route is a film choice. Luke says he went outside and wept bitterly.

## 4. Camera axis and coverage

**Proposed rule:** Keep the principal Peter–Jesus eyeline axis running diagonally from the south-east fire-side position to the north-west far-side route. Keep all dialogue and look/reverse-look cameras on the west/south-west side of that axis unless a deliberate axis change is planned and clearly re-established.

Coverage priorities:

- One wide master establishes the gate, fire, inner door, Peter, and the far-side route.
- Medium shots for the three accusers must retain the fire's screen position and Peter's relationship to the gate.
- Peter's close-ups should preserve his eyeline toward the far-side route.
- Jesus' turn should be filmed from the same side of the axis.
- The Peter reaction and Jesus look must match direction and screen position.
- Do not horizontally flip generated footage to repair an axis mistake.

Exact screen-left/screen-right assignments should be finalized only after a chosen master composition is drawn and tested.

## 5. Lighting and atmosphere

- Night ambience remains cool-neutral, not saturated blue.
- The fire is a practical, low source with restrained amber light.
- Peter's face should be readable when the servant woman notices him.
- Jesus should remain visible on the far side without an artificial spotlight.
- The rooster's crow causes no lighting change.
- Do not use smoke, fog, sparks, or crowd movement as decorative spectacle.
